# Contributing to weGov

weGov is meant to belong to everyone, so it should be shaped by everyone from the very start. You don't need to be a programmer. The most valuable contributions right now are **ideas, challenges and lived experience**.

Until weGov can govern itself, the project uses GitHub to discuss and decide. Once the platform is running, the project will move onto weGov itself.

---

## Ways to contribute

### 1. Question the structure

This is the most important contribution right now. If you think part of the [Manifesto](MANIFESTO.md) or the [Layers design](docs/layers.md) is wrong, unfair, missing something or won't work where you live, **say so**.

1. Check the [Open Questions register](docs/open-questions.md) to see whether your question is already there.
2. If it isn't, [open a new issue](../../issues/new/choose) and choose **"Question the structure"**.
3. If it is, add your view to the existing issue.

There are no stupid questions and no sacred cows. Challenging the foundations makes them stronger.

### 2. Propose a change

If you have a specific wording change or a new idea for the manifesto or a design document:

1. [Open an issue](../../issues/new/choose) and choose **"Propose a change"**, so it can be discussed first.
2. Once there is some agreement, open a pull request with the change, or ask someone to help you do it.

### 3. Share how decisions are made where you live

How does your village, town, union, co-op or community make decisions today? What works and what doesn't? Real experience from around the world, especially from places that are usually ignored, is how weGov will avoid designing only for the rich and connected. Use the **"Share your community's experience"** issue type.

### 4. Translate

The manifesto needs to exist in every language. Translations will live in `translations/<language-code>/`. Open an issue to say which language you're working on, so effort isn't duplicated.

### 5. Build

Platform code doesn't exist yet. Design work comes first. If you have skills in cryptography, SMS/USSD and voice systems, distributed systems, accessibility or participatory democracy, join the design discussions now. Your input will shape what gets built.

---

## How decisions are made, for now

1. **Anyone** can raise a question or propose a change through an issue.
2. Discussion happens in the open, in the issue.
3. Changes are made through pull requests that link to the issue.
4. A change is merged once there is broad agreement and no strong, unresolved objection.
5. Every decision and its reasoning stays public.

This is a temporary process. Changing it is itself an open question (see [F-01](docs/open-questions.md#foundations)).

## How we treat each other

We follow the principles of the [Manifesto](MANIFESTO.md):

- **Equal voice.** Everyone's contribution counts, whatever their background, language, education or experience.
- **Dignity.** Disagree with ideas, never attack people. No harassment, discrimination or threats.
- **Plain language.** Write so that people reading in their second or third language can follow. Explain any jargon.
- **Assume good faith.** Most disagreements are honest differences in experience.
- **Nonviolence.** weGov will not be used to organise harm against anyone.

Behaviour that breaks these principles can be reported by opening an issue or contacting the maintainers. Maintainers hold that role on behalf of the community and can be challenged too.
